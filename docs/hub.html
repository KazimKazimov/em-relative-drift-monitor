What it is

A detector for "which countries have moved a lot relative to peers" that does not depend on a lookback window. Instead of a 1-month or 3-month change, it accumulates beta-adjusted daily surprises and lets the data decide where the move started. Four views:

  1. Ranked: 30 countries sorted by a signed CUSUM statistic, with bp since onset, days since onset, and the larger swing the move sits inside.
  2. Pair matrix: 30×30 heatmap, cell (i, j) = CUSUM of i's yield change against j's with a recursive pair beta.
  3. Scanner: names and pairs whose historical analogs say "continue" or "revert" with the probability, horizon, pooling and conditioning you choose.
  4. Backtest: country-level long/short with three rules, beta-adjusted sizing, equity curves, parameter sweeps, and attribution of PnL to the state each trade was entered in.

Data in the prototype is synthetic (30 names, ~6 years, with a "true dynamics" knob from trending to reverting). The engine is data-agnostic: anything that becomes a T×N matrix of daily changes works.

Method

1. Beta-adjusted residual

For each country i and day t, Δyi,t is regressed on the common factor ft (cross-sectional mean of Δy, or a supplied index) with a recursive least-squares beta using a forgetting factor λ = 0.51/half-life. The residual ei,t = Δyi,t − βi,t−1 ft is standardized by an EWMA of its own variance to give zi,t. Toggle: cross-sectional demeaning instead of beta.

2. Two-sided CUSUM

S+ = max(0, S+ + z − k), S− = max(0, S− − z − k). The signed statistic S+ − S− ranks the cross-section; positive = yield rising vs peers (cheapening). An alarm fires when either side exceeds h and both reset. The onset of the current move is the last day the signed statistic was at or below zero; days since onset is the lookback the data chose.

3. Swing (zigzag) layer

CUSUM's drift allowance k makes it forget slow moves. The un-reset cumulative residual Rt = Σe is tracked with a zigzag: a turning point is confirmed when R reverses by more than ZZ × (250d residual vol) × √20. For each name: swing start, bp since the last confirmed turn, peak excursion, pullback from peak, and the swing's percentile against the name's completed swings. The detail view says whether the CUSUM move is with or against the larger swing.

4. Pair matrix

Same CUSUM on Δyi − bij Δyj with a recursive pair beta, for all ordered pairs. Not exactly antisymmetric in beta mode (bij ≠ 1/bji). Country net score gi = meanj(Sij − Sji)/2.

5. Analogs

An episode is the first day a series' signed statistic crosses a |S| bucket (0.5 steps) in a given direction; it stores the forward residual at 5/10/20/40 days, the run age bin at crossing (≤5, 6–20, >20 d) and the vol-regime bin (60d/250d residual vol <0.8, 0.8–1.25, >1.25). P(continue) for today's reading is the share of analogs whose forward residual kept the drift's sign. Pooling: all names, same region, similar rating bucket, or similar duration bucket; optional conditioning on age and vol bins. If a pool is thinner than the minimum, it widens automatically and is flagged.

6. Backtest

Signal at close t, PnL on t+1, 60-day warm-up. Rules: (a) re-rank daily top-K by net score; (b) enter |g| > h/2, exit < h/4; (c) analog probabilities, where each day's probability uses only episodes whose horizon completed before that day, trades are held for the horizon, ranked by edge and capped at a maximum open count, and the momentum and reversion books are tracked separately. Sizing: 1/β per leg (net factor beta zero ex ante, PnL on raw Δy) or equal weight with residual PnL. Attribution tags each trade with entry |S| bucket, run age, with/against swing, vol regime and book, and reports n, hit rate, mean bp, t-stat per bucket plus the average PnL path by day in trade.

Findings on synthetic data

  • Follow vs fade is entirely a property of the data, not the detector: zero-cost Sharpe flips sign between the trending and reverting generator settings, as it should.
  • Costs decide the exit rule. Daily re-ranking of top-K churned both books negative at 1 bp; the alarm-threshold exit and the horizon-hold kept most of the edge.
  • Attribution on a mildly trending sample: entries 6–20 days after onset earned +8.7 bp (t = 4.2); entries ≤5 days earned +1.2 bp; entries on the alarm day (|S| ≥ 5) lost ~30 bp. With-swing momentum trades +5.4 bp (t = 3.8) vs against-swing +3.9 bp (t = 1.9).
  • Beta-neutral 1/β sizing costs a little Sharpe versus residual booking (7.1 vs 7.3 on one sample), the expected leak from beta estimation error.
  • Region or rating pooling with context matching raises top-row P(continue) from ~65% on hundreds of analogs to ~75–80% on tens; the asterisk tells you when the pool had to widen.

None of these numbers transfer to real markets; the point is that the machinery responds the right way to known dynamics.

Data spec for real markets

The engine needs, per asset class, a daily return in profit direction, a hedge factor, and a cost estimate. Common to all: closes on one calendar with a stale-day flag (the CUSUM must skip stale days), universe membership history, a timestamp convention so signal day t strictly precedes PnL day t+1, and a per-name cost table.

Roadmap

Signal quality

  1. Swing-aware analogs: condition P(continue) on with/against the larger swing and swing age.
  2. Kalman random-walk betas and a 2–3 factor model per asset class (replaces the half-life with process noise; separates "cheap vs EM" from "cheap vs its dollar or commodity beta").
  3. Carry-adjusted residuals before FX or CDS go in.
  4. Multi-asset confirmation: a per-country count of instruments alarming the same way.

Trusting the backtest

  1. Walk-forward holdout: fit the grid on the first half, score on the second.
  2. PnL attribution by entry state (done).
  3. Exposure diagnostics: realised factor beta, region and rating tilts of the live book.
  4. Wilson confidence intervals on P(continue) next to each probability.

Usability

  1. Daily change view: fresh alarms, threshold crossings, newly confirmed swing turns.
  2. Event calendar overlay: central bank meetings, auctions, index rebalances, elections.
  3. Export the scanner's passing trades with hedge ratios into the portfolio engine.

Suggested order: 1, 8 and 7 on the current prototype; 5 before anyone sizes a position; 2–4 once real data is in, at which point the engine should move out of the browser into MATLAB or Python and the page become a viewer over its output.
